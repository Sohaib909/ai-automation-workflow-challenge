Step by step, with one example

We follow one made-up campaign the whole way: Harbour Lights Foundation's Winter Appeal, asking people to give £10 a month to help young people facing homelessness.

1

Brief: capture what we know

The account team fills in one short form: who we're talking to, what's stopping them, the offer, and the facts we're allowed to use. The client's brand voice and the phrases they never want used are saved once and reused for every campaign.

Person

Writes the brief and lists the provable facts. AI can only use these facts.

Safety check

Required details must be filled in. Every change to the brief is saved as a new version.

Example: Audience is past donors aged 30–60. Their worry: "my money disappears into admin." Fact we can use: "£25 funds one night of emergency accommodation." Never say: "100% of your donation."

2

Ideas: lots of directions, quickly

AI suggests several genuinely different campaign ideas. Each one comes at the problem from a different angle, so we don't get five versions of the same thought. Each idea arrives as a short creative brief with a headline thought, an opening line, a visual direction, a video idea and what we'd be testing.

AI

Drafts the ideas and scores each one on things like clarity, originality and brand fit, explaining every score.

Safety check

Flags made-up numbers, banned phrases and ideas too similar to ones we've already had.

Person

A strategist approves, rejects with a reason, or rates each idea. That feedback steers the next batch.

Example: The AI offers "One Night, Counted" (show exactly what £25 pays for), a myth-buster about admin costs, and a day-in-the-life story told from a support worker's view. The strategist approves "One Night, Counted".

3

Make: one approved idea becomes everything we need

Once an idea is approved, the same thinking flows into each type of content, so the ad, the video and the landing page all tell the same story.

Ad words

  • Writes several versions: opening lines, headlines, main text and a button
  • Checks length limits, banned phrases and unproven claims
  • Copywriter edits, asks for rewrites, approves

Image ads

  • Creates the picture or scene
  • The real logo and headline are added from a brand template, never drawn by AI
  • Designer picks, polishes and approves

Video

  • Writes the script scene by scene
  • Chooses a real creator or an AI presenter
  • First edit: cuts pauses, adds captions, logo and music, makes each size
  • Editor fine-tunes and approves

Landing page

  • Plans the sections and writes them, matching the ad
  • No invented reviews, links work, works on phones
  • Previewed and approved before publishing

Example: The ad opens with "£25. One night. One safe bed." A 20-second video shows a support worker making up a bed. The landing page leads with the same line and a £10/month sign-up.

4

Approve: one place to sign things off

Everything waiting for a decision sits in one queue, next to its brief and safety-check notes. If someone edits an approved piece, it goes back into the queue, so nothing changes quietly after sign-off.

Person

Approves, rejects or asks for changes. Every decision is recorded with who made it and when.

Safety check

Anything with a serious problem, such as a banned phrase, can't be approved until it's fixed.

5

Launch: our team goes live

A media buyer takes the approved, ready-to-use pieces and sets the ads live. AI never launches, pauses or changes ads, and never touches budgets on its own.

6

Watch: every client's results in one dashboard

Each morning, results from every client's ad account are collected into one dashboard covering money spent, people reached, clicks, donations or sales, and cost per result. It can be filtered by client, campaign and date.

Safety check

Bad or duplicate rows are caught. Missing numbers show as "not available", never as zero.

AI

Writes a plain-English summary. Any figure it mentions is checked against the real data.

Person

An account manager reads it before anything goes to the client.

7

Advise: an assistant that never sleeps, but never acts alone

The ads assistant watches for things a busy team can miss: costs creeping up, an ad people are tired of seeing, a sudden spike in spend, an ad spending money with no results, or an ad doing unusually well. Each alert shows the exact numbers behind it and a suggested next step.

AI

Spots the pattern, explains it simply and suggests what to check.

Person

The media buyer accepts, rejects or dismisses each suggestion, then makes any change themselves.

Example: "The £25 One Night ad costs about half as much per donation as the rest of the campaign this week." The media buyer accepts. Separately: "The night-shelter video is being seen too often and fewer people click." That flags a creative refresh.

8

Learn: results go back into the next ideas

When a person accepts a lesson like "showing exactly what money buys works for this audience", it's handed to the Idea Generator for that client. The next batch of ideas builds on what is actually working, and says so.

Example: The next round for Harbour Lights opens with new ideas built around concrete, countable impact. The approved lesson is shown alongside them so the strategist can see why.

The AI tools we'd use, and why

No single AI is best at everything, so each job gets the tool that does it best. The system can switch a job to a better or cheaper tool at any time without rebuilding anything.

Campaign ideasClaude Opus 5.5Also: OpenAI GPT, for a second set of ideas

Thinks well about a long brief and the audience, and keeps to the brand's rules.

Ad words, scripts, landing pagesClaude Sonnet 5.5Also: OpenAI GPT

Writes naturally in the brand's voice and sticks to the facts it's given.

Quick checks and summariesClaude Haiku 5.5Also: smaller OpenAI models

Fast and inexpensive for the many small checks behind every piece.

Pictures for image adsOpenAI image model or Google ImagenAlso: FLUX, a lower-cost open model

Creates the scene; our own template adds the real headline, button and logo so the words are always right.

Presenter videosHeyGen + ElevenLabsAlso: Google Veo, Runway or Kling for extra scenes

Licensed presenters and natural voices. For fundraising, real people on camera stay the first choice.

Video editingOpenAI Whisper + editing automationAlso: Creatomate, Descript

Writes out every spoken word with timings, so captions and cuts are accurate.

Ad resultsMeta's own reporting connection (read-only)Also: n8n for scheduling, Looker Studio for clients

Numbers are calculated exactly by the system; AI only explains them.

In today's demo the writing agents run on a free AI service (Ollama Cloud) so anyone can try it at no cost. Claude or OpenAI plug into the same slot. Check current versions and prices before choosing.

What AI never does on its own

  • Publish an ad, a video or a web page
  • Change a budget, pause a campaign or edit targeting
  • Invent statistics, reviews, testimonials or quotes
  • Send anything to a client without a person reading it first

How we'd roll it out

First 2 weeks

Ideas, ad words and the results dashboard working on a few real briefs. These are quick wins that everything else builds on.

By day 30

Results collected automatically for every client each morning. Alerts sent to the team. Scripts and image ads added.

By day 60

Video editing help, landing pages and a first trial of AI-presented video.

By day 90

All nine areas connected into one loop, with costs and quality tracked per client.

What's next

Learn the real work

Spend time with the team and collect past briefs and winning ads, so every improvement is tested against real examples.

Connect the real data

Pull every client's Meta results automatically each night, with alerts sent to the team's chat.

Best AI for each job

Move writing to Claude, compare results with the team, and keep whatever performs best.

Finish the video steps

Connect presenter video and automatic editing for one pilot client first.
